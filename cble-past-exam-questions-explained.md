**Part 141 sets the general rules for entering imported merchandise: who may make entry, within what time, under what power of attorney, and in how many entries.** The most important deadline is in 19 CFR 141.5: merchandise must be entered within **15 calendar days** after landing (or after arrival at the port of destination, if moved in bond). The rest of this page puts every Part 141 time limit in one table and lists the distinctions that are easy to mix up under exam pressure.

## Quick-reference table: Part 141 deadlines and time limits

| What | Time limit | Count as | Citation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry of merchandise (general rule) | Within **15 calendar days** after landing from the vessel, aircraft or vehicle, or after arrival at the destination port if transported in bond | Calendar days | § 141.5 |
| Undeliverable articles exemption from entry | Merchandise returned within **45 days** of departure from the U.S. (one of five required certifications) | Days | § 141.4(c)(1)(ii) |
| Actual owner's declaration (CF 3347) or actual owner's superseding bond (CF 301) | Filed within **90 days** from the time of entry | Days | § 141.20(a)(1), (a)(2) |
| Power of attorney issued by a partnership | Limited to **2 years** from execution. All other powers of attorney may be for an unlimited period | Years | § 141.34 |
| Revocation of a power of attorney | Effective on written notice **given to and received by CBP** | Receipt | § 141.35 |
| Split shipment: all portions at the same port | Succeeding portions must arrive within **10 calendar days** of the first portion | Calendar days | § 141.57(b)(3) |
| Split shipment: importer's notice | As soon as the importer knows of the split, and **before the entry summary is filed** | Event-based | § 141.57(c) |
| Split shipment: entry summary | Within **10 working days** (from time of entry, or from first release under special permit) | Working days | § 141.57(g)(1), (g)(2) |
| Unassembled/disassembled entity: arrival of all portions ("hold all") | Within **15 calendar days** after unlading of the first portion (or arrival at destination port if in bond) | Calendar days | § 141.58(b)(4)(i) |
| Unassembled/disassembled entity: arrival of all portions (incremental release) | Within **10 calendar days** after release of the first portion | Calendar days | § 141.58(b)(4)(ii) |
| Unassembled/disassembled entity: application to file a single entry | No later than **5 working days before** arrival of the first conveyance. Port director answers within **3 working days** of receipt | Working days | § 141.58(c) |
| Unassembled/disassembled entity: entry summary | Within **10 working days** | Working days | § 141.58(g)(1), (g)(2) |
| Statistical documentation produced under bond | Within **50 days** after the entry summary (or entry, if none) is required to be filed. For warehouse entries: **2 months** after withdrawal, but an invoice within 50 days after the warehouse entry summary is required | Days / months | § 141.61(e)(2)(i), (ii) |
| Withdrawal from warehouse for consumption | Requirements and estimated duties completed within **60 days** from presentation of the CBP Form 7501, or the request is considered abandoned | Days | § 141.68(g) |
| Overcarried merchandise returned to port | Returned within **90 days** after removal to keep the rates in effect at the original entry | Days | § 141.69(c) |
| Installment shipments on one invoice | Installments arrive within **10 consecutive days** | Consecutive days | § 141.82(a) |
| Missing invoice for installment series | Liquidated damages accrue per entry if **more than 6 months** pass without the invoice | Months | § 141.82(c) |

**The pattern to remember:** the first-level entry window (§ 141.5) and the split/unassembled arrival windows count **calendar** days. The entry summary filing windows in §§ 141.57(g) and 141.58(g) count **working** days. The general 10-working-day entry summary rule sits in Part 142, not Part 141. Look up the exact subsection in your edition before relying on it.

## Right to make entry (Subpart B): quick reference

| Situation | Rule | Citation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Importation by common carrier | Evidence of right to make entry must be filed. Accepted types include a bill of lading or air waybill presented by its holder (endorsed if required), a carrier-certified extract, a certified duplicate, a carrier's certificate, or a blanket carrier's release order | § 141.11(a)(1)-(5) |
| Shipping receipt in place of a bill of lading | Accepted only with a carrier's certificate, or if entry is made by the actual consignee in person or by a duly authorized agent | § 141.11(a)(6) |
| Merchandise released directly to the carrier | Delivery by the carrier to the person filing the entry summary with estimated duties is deemed the required certification. CBP makes no representation about the right to possession | § 141.11(b) |
| Importation by other than a common carrier | **Possession** at the time of arrival is sufficient evidence | § 141.12 |
| Abandoned or salvaged merchandise | Underwriters or salvors without a bill of lading must show satisfactory evidence of their right to act | § 141.13 |
| Deceased or insolvent consignee | Executor, receiver or similar representative must produce a duly endorsed bill of lading, air waybill, carrier's certificate or duplicate showing him to be the consignee for Customs purposes | § 141.14 |
| No bill of lading available | Port director may accept a bond for its production on CBP Form 301 (conditions in § 113.69). **No bond** is needed if entry is made on a carrier's certificate or certified duplicate | § 141.15(a), (b) |
| Nonresident consignee | **Has** the right to make entry, but any bond must have a resident corporate surety | § 141.17 |
| Nonresident corporation | May not enter for consumption unless it has a resident agent in the port's State authorized to accept service of process, **and** files a CBP Form 301 bond with a resident corporate surety | § 141.18(a), (b) |

## Powers of attorney (Subpart C): quick reference

| Rule | Detail | Citation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | May cover a specified part of, or all of, the principal's Customs business | § 141.31(a) |
| Minors | A power of attorney to a minor shall not be accepted | § 141.31(c) |
| Form | CBP Form 5291 may be used. Otherwise it must be a general power with unlimited authority, or a limited one explicit in its terms | § 141.32 |
| Noncommercial single shipment | An individual who is not a regular importer may appoint an unpaid agent by statement on the invoice or an attached paper | § 141.33 |
| Duration | Partnership: max **2 years**. All others: unlimited | § 141.34 |
| Revocation | At any time, by written notice given to and received by CBP | § 141.35 |
| Nonresident principal | Agent must be a **resident** and authorized to accept service of process | § 141.36 |
| Resident corporation | **No** power of attorney needed if the signer is known to CBP to be the president, vice president, treasurer or secretary | § 141.38 |
| Partnership | Must name all members. One member may execute it for all of its Customs business | § 141.39(a)(1) |
| Subagents: resident principal | Holder **cannot** appoint a subagent, except to file Electronic Export Information. A subagent cannot delegate | § 141.43(a) |
| Subagents: nonresident principal | Only if the original power expressly authorizes it, and the subagent must be a resident authorized to accept service of process | § 141.43(b) |
| Customhouse broker | Power may be granted to the broker to act through its licensed officers or authorized employees (see Part 111) | § 141.43(c) |
| Where filed | Specify the Center or ports where valid, or all CBP ports. File with CBP at the port of entry or electronically, unless exempted by § 141.46 | § 141.44 |
| Broker's power of attorney | Broker must obtain a valid power **before** transacting Customs business in the principal's name. **Not required to file** it with CBP. Retain it with books and papers | § 141.46 |

## Easy-to-mix-up distinctions, with exact subsections

These are rules where the text draws a line that is easy to blur when you are searching a printed book against the clock. I have not checked how often each one appears on past exams. Check them against the CBP past exams and answer keys to see which ones your sitting emphasizes.

**1. Calendar days vs working days.** Entry must be made within 15 **calendar** days (§ 141.5). The split shipment entry summary is due within 10 **working** days (§ 141.57(g)). The unassembled-entity application is due 5 **working** days before arrival (§ 141.58(c)). Read the unit before you read the number.

**2. Split shipments vs unassembled entities: 10 vs 15 days.** A split shipment's later portions must arrive within 10 calendar days of the first (§ 141.57(b)(3)). For an unassembled entity, it is 15 calendar days under "hold all" (§ 141.58(b)(4)(i)) but 10 calendar days under incremental release (§ 141.58(b)(4)(ii)). A split shipment is divided by the **carrier acting on its own**. An unassembled entity is one that cannot, due to its size or nature, be shipped on a single conveyance.

**3. Who splits a shipment.** The importer must keep evidence that the **carrier**, not the shipper or importer, split the shipment (§ 141.57(j)(2)).

**4. Quota merchandise is excluded.** Quota or visa merchandise is excluded from incremental release for split shipments (§ 141.57(k)(1)) and entirely excluded from the unassembled-entity procedure (§ 141.58(j)).

**5. Partnership vs everyone else on power of attorney duration.** Only a partnership's power of attorney is capped at 2 years (§ 141.34). A corporation's or individual's may be unlimited.

**6. Who needs a power of attorney at all.** A resident corporation does not need one if the signer is known to CBP as president, vice president, treasurer, or secretary (§ 141.38). A job title outside that list does not qualify under that sentence.

**7. A broker must have it, but need not file it.** § 141.46 requires the broker to obtain a valid power of attorney before acting, but says the broker is **not required to file** it with CBP, in contrast to the general filing rule in § 141.44.

**8. Revocation takes effect on receipt.** Written notice must be "given to and received by" CBP (§ 141.35).

**9. Nonresident principal vs nonresident consignee.** A nonresident principal's power of attorney is accepted only if the agent is a resident authorized to accept service of process (§ 141.36). A nonresident consignee has the right to make entry, but the bond needs a resident corporate surety (§ 141.17). A nonresident **corporation** has extra requirements (§ 141.18).

**10. A nonnegotiable bill of lading cannot be endorsed over.** The named consignee may not endorse a nonnegotiable bill of lading or air waybill to give someone else the right to make entry (§ 141.11(a)(1)).

**11. Possession counts only for non-common-carrier shipments.** The "possession is sufficient evidence" rule is in § 141.12, which applies to importations by other than common carrier. For common carrier importations, § 141.11 requires the listed documents.

**12. When a bond for the bill of lading is not needed.** No bond is required where entry is made on a carrier's certificate or a certified duplicate (§ 141.15(a)).

**13. Payment to a broker does not discharge the importer.** If the broker fails to pay the duties, the importer remains liable (§ 141.1(b)(1)).

**14. Which rate of duty applies.** The rate in effect at the **time of entry** (§ 141.69), as defined in § 141.68. Warehouse entries use the rate when withdrawal for consumption is made (§ 141.69(a)).

**15. Recall.** The importer may recall entry and entry summary documentation any time **before** the effective time of entry (§ 141.67).

**16. Declaration need not be under oath.** The consignee's declaration under § 141.19(a) does not have to be sworn. For a partnership, any partner may sign it. For a corporation, any officer may.

## FAQ

**What is the entry deadline in 19 CFR Part 141?**
Merchandise requiring entry must be entered within 15 calendar days after landing, or after arrival at the port of destination if moved in bond (§ 141.5). Merchandise not timely entered is treated under § 4.37, § 122.50 or § 123.10, depending on the mode.

**How long can a power of attorney last?**
A partnership's is limited to 2 years. All others may be unlimited (§ 141.34).

**Does a customs broker file the power of attorney with CBP?**
No. The broker must obtain it before acting, but is not required to file it with CBP, and keeps it with the broker's books and papers (§ 141.46).

**What proves the right to make entry?**
For common carrier importations, one of the document types in § 141.11(a). For other importations, possession at the time of arrival (§ 141.12).

**How long does the actual owner have to file a declaration?**
Within 90 days from the time of entry (§ 141.20(a)(1)).

**Is the exam open book?**
Yes, with printed materials only. Electronic devices are not allowed (source: [ST&R course page](https://www.strtrade.com/training/customs-broker-exam-preparation-course)).
